The platform

Every style is the same four layers. Only the shell changes, so one electronics design, one harness spec and one test procedure cover the whole line.

Harness and transducersLow-voltage wiring, transducer pockets, the hem frequency port and the pod connectorGarment factory installs; EMS partner supplies the harness
Frequency moduleRemovable pod with battery, amplifier and USB-C charging, plus the meter plug-inEMS partner, shipped to the garment factory finished and tested
5 volts, maxNothing in the jacket runs above 5 V. No mains, no high-voltage parts, ever.
No copper on skinEvery trace is sealed under film or lining. Plated fabric can contain nickel, so it never sits in direct, prolonged skin contact.
Pod out to washAll electronics leave with the pod. What stays in the jacket must survive a cold delicate wash.
No health claimsLabels, hangtags and marketing describe audio and feel only. No medical or therapeutic wording.

Electronics

Reference spec for the EMS partner. It describes what the module has to do; the partner proposes parts and we approve them.

Assembly

In order. Gates are where the unit gets checked before moving on.

  1. Cut

    Cut shell, interlining and lining panels from the marker for the size. Laser-cut copper traces from the conductive fabric using the style's trace template, scaled to the size block.

  2. Bond the copper

    Lay traces on the interlining using the placement jig and heat-press with TPU film. Use the temperature, time and pressure from the film supplier's datasheet and record them per batch.

    Gate: peel test on one off-cut per roll

  3. Seal the traces

    Cap every trace with a second layer of TPU film so no copper is exposed on the body side. Exposed traces on outer shells (London Calling, Riot) are sealed on both faces.

  4. Sew the shell

    Construct the shell. Where a trace crosses a seam, bridge it with a flexible jumper soldered or crimped on each side; never sew through a trace.

  5. Install pockets and harness

    Sew transducer pockets to the interlining at the marked positions. Route the harness through the lining channels to the pod pocket (inside left hem) and the frequency port (right hem). Strain-relieve every termination.

  6. Test before lining

    Run the pre-lining checks: every trace and harness run for continuity, no shorts between neighbours, and the impedance of each channel within the planned range.

    Gate: pre-lining checklist complete

  7. Close up

    Insert transducers, close pockets, attach lining, then cuffs, hem and collar per style. Install the port gasket and pod connector.

  8. Power-up test

    Seat a tested pod, play the sweep track and confirm every transducer moves, nothing buzzes or rattles, and the meter plug-in responds.

    Gate: final checklist complete

  9. Finish and pack

    Remove the pod, press the jacket, attach labels and hangtag, then pack the jacket, pod in its pouch, cable and quick-start card.

Quality checks

One checklist per unit. Ticks are saved in this browser per serial number, so the line lead can keep a few units open at once.

0/0 checks done

Batch tests

  • Wash test: one unit per 50, five cold delicate cycles with the pod removed, then repeat the pre-lining electrical checks.
  • Battery: one pod per 100 fully discharged at moderate level to confirm runtime; record the result.
  • Final inspection: AQL sampling at General Level II, 2.5 for major and 4.0 for minor defects, before the batch ships.

Sizing

Body measurements for the base blocks. Each style's tech pack adds its own ease. Transducer positions and harness length grade with the size.

Men'sChest (in)Chest (cm)
Women'sBust (in)Bust (cm)
  • Chest transducers sit at a fixed fraction of the half-chest width, so they stay centred on the body in every size.
  • Harness length grades about 4 cm per size; the EMS partner supplies one harness length per size.
  • Garment measurement tolerance: ±1 cm on chest and length, ±0.5 cm on sleeve opening.

Compliance

What has to be in place before units ship. Confirm the exact list for each destination with a test lab.

AreaWhat's needed
BatteriesUN38.3 test summary and safety data sheet for the cell or pack, IEC 62133 report, and lithium battery shipping marks for batteries contained in or packed with equipment.
Electronics (US)FCC Part 15 assessment for the module as a digital device. Any wireless version uses a pre-certified radio module.
Electronics (EU, UK)CE and UKCA marking, RoHS, WEEE and battery-regulation registration and labelling where we sell.
MaterialsNickel-release limits for any metal that touches skin, REACH substances, and California Prop 65 review of leather, trims and plated fabric.
Textile labelsFibre content, country of origin, care instructions including pod removal, and the brand identifier.
ClaimsCopy describes audio and feel only. No medical, therapeutic or wellness claims on labels, packaging, the site or the campaign.
